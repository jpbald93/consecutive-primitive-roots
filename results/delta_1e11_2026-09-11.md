# δ(10¹¹): fourth data point, and my pre-registered prediction failed — 2026-09-11

## Result

Full independent census to 10¹¹ (200 shards on jack, 32 cores, ~7 min wall):

```
n_primes  = 4,118,054,810      (π(10¹¹) = 4,118,054,813, minus p = 2, 3, 5 — exact)
n_artin   = 1,539,964,486
density   = 0.3739543          (Artin's constant 0.3739558 — agrees to 6 dp)
n_pairs   = 4,118,054,809
table     = [[1599542647, 978547677], [978547677, 561416808]]
delta     = -0.014998215680
chi2      = 926,341.9
gaps 20/60: 194,296,748 pairs, 0 doubly-Artin
```

**The theorem holds at 10¹¹**: zero doubly-Artin pairs among 194,296,748
consecutive pairs with gap 20 or 60. That is an 88-fold larger test set than
at 10⁹ and a 9-fold increase over 10¹⁰.

The prime count landing exactly on π(10¹¹) − 3, and the Artin density
reproducing Artin's constant to six decimals over 4.1 billion primes, are
strong independent checks that the census is sound.

## The pre-registered prediction was wrong

Before running (see `PREREGISTERED_1e11.md`) I recorded three models:

| model | predicted δ(10¹¹) | actual error |
|---|---|---|
| geometric increments (r = 0.747) | −0.015159 | **off by 10 SE** |
| linear in log x | −0.015306 | off by 20 SE |
| δ → 0 | less negative than −0.014723 | ruled out |
| **actual** | **−0.014998216** | SE = 1.6 × 10⁻⁵ |

The δ → 0 branch is ruled out over this range — δ continued to grow more
negative. But my geometric extrapolation also failed, by 10 standard errors:
the increments are decaying *faster* than geometric.

I also want to record an error I made in reading the output: an early script
of mine described the increment ratio as "stable ~0.61" by averaging 0.747 and
0.471. Those are not stable, and the average was meaningless. The ratio is
clearly decreasing.

## Four decades

| x | δ(x) | increment | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10⁸ | −0.013360 | — | — |
| 10⁹ | −0.014140159 | −0.000780 | — |
| 10¹⁰ | −0.014723293 | −0.000583 | 0.747 |
| 10¹¹ | −0.014998216 | −0.000275 | 0.471 |

Two models tested and both found wanting:

- **Pure geometric decay of increments:** ratio is not constant (0.747 → 0.471).
- **δ ∼ c/log x:** δ·log x drifts monotonically (−0.246, −0.293, −0.339, −0.380),
  so this is a poor fit too.

## Honest conclusion

δ continues to grow more negative through 10¹¹, with increments shrinking
faster than geometric. This is **evidence against** simple 1/log-type decay to
zero over 10⁸–10¹¹, and **consistent with** convergence to a small nonzero
limit somewhere near −0.015, but the increment law is not identified and no
limit should be asserted in the manuscript.

Recommended manuscript wording: report all four values, note that δ
strengthens monotonically with decelerating increments across four decades and
4.1 billion pairs, state that this does not support the δ → 0 branch of the
separable-channel heuristic over the measured range, and explicitly decline to
claim a limit. The Persistence Conjecture (δ < 0 for all sufficiently large x)
is supported; the stronger claim of a nonzero limit is not established.
